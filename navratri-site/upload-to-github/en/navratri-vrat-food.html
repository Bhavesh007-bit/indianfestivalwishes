What to Eat and Avoid During the Navratri Fast, Plus 2 Easy Recipes

Many people fast during Navratri, either for all nine days or on the first and last day. Rules vary from family to family, so follow your own household's tradition. Below are the practices most homes follow.

What is usually eaten

Buckwheat flour (kuttu), water chestnut flour (singhara), amaranth (rajgira), barnyard millet (samak) and sago (sabudana) take the place of grains. Vegetables such as potato, sweet potato, colocasia, pumpkin and bottle gourd, all fruits, milk, curd, paneer, makhana, peanuts and dry fruits are eaten. Rock salt (sendha namak) is used in place of regular salt.

What is usually avoided

Wheat, rice, lentils, onion, garlic, regular salt, meat and alcohol are avoided. Some families also skip turmeric and certain spices.

Recipe 1: Sabudana khichdi

Rinse one cup of sabudana and soak it in the same amount of water for 5 to 6 hours, so the pearls turn soft and stay separate. Heat a spoon of ghee in a pan, add cumin, green chillies and one boiled, chopped potato, and sauté for two minutes. Add the sabudana, half a cup of coarsely ground roasted peanuts and rock salt to taste. Stir on low heat for 4 to 5 minutes until the pearls turn translucent. Finish with lemon juice and coriander.

Recipe 2: Kuttu puri

Mix one cup of kuttu flour with one boiled, mashed potato, a little rock salt and enough water to make a soft dough. The potato keeps the puris from cracking while you roll them. Roll out small puris and fry them in hot ghee or oil until golden on both sides. Serve with dry potato sabzi or curd.

Look after your health

Eating a lot of fried food at once after a full day without food can upset your stomach. Have fruit, coconut water and buttermilk through the day. If you have diabetes, blood pressure or any other condition, or if you are pregnant, talk to your doctor before fasting.
